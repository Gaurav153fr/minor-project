## Unit-I

### 1. (a) Define bond order using a suitable example. [4 Marks]
- **Answer:** 
  **Bond Order Definition:** Bond order is defined as half the difference between the number of bonding electrons ($N_b$) and the number of anti-bonding electrons ($N_a$) in a molecule:
  $$\text{Bond Order} = \frac{N_b - N_a}{2}$$
  **Example ($H_2$ molecule):**
  - Total electrons in $H_2 = 2$
  - Electronic configuration: $(\sigma_{1s})^2 (\sigma^*_{1s})^0$
  - Number of bonding electrons ($N_b$) = 2, Anti-bonding ($N_a$) = 0
  - $\text{Bond Order} = \frac{2 - 0}{2} = 1$ (Represents a stable single covalent bond between the two hydrogen atoms).

### 1. (b) Draw the Molecular Orbital diagram of $O_2$ and $NO$. Predict their bond order and magnetic character. [4+4 Marks]
- **Answer:**
  **1. Oxygen Molecule ($O_2$):**
  - Total electrons = $8 + 8 = 16$
  - Configuration: $\sigma_{1s}^2 \sigma^*_{1s}^2 \sigma_{2s}^2 \sigma^*_{2s}^2 \sigma_{2p_z}^2 (\pi_{2p_x}^2 = \pi_{2p_y}^2) (\pi^*_{2p_x}^1 = \pi^*_{2p_y}^1)$
  - Bonding electrons ($N_b$) = 10, Anti-bonding ($N_a$) = 6
  - $\text{Bond Order} = \frac{10 - 6}{2} = 2$
  - **Magnetic Character:** Paramagnetic (due to the presence of 2 unpaired electrons in degenerate $\pi^*_{2p_x}$ and $\pi^*_{2p_y}$ orbitals).

  **2. Nitric Oxide Molecule ($NO$):**
  - Total electrons = $7 + 8 = 15$
  - Configuration: $\sigma_{1s}^2 \sigma^*_{1s}^2 \sigma_{2s}^2 \sigma^*_{2s}^2 (\pi_{2p_x}^2 = \pi_{2p_y}^2) \sigma_{2p_z}^2 (\pi^*_{2p_x}^1 = \pi^*_{2p_y}^0)$
  - Bonding electrons ($N_b$) = 10, Anti-bonding ($N_a$) = 5
  - $\text{Bond Order} = \frac{10 - 5}{2} = 2.5$
  - **Magnetic Character:** Paramagnetic (due to 1 unpaired electron in the $\pi^*_{2p_x}$ anti-bonding orbital).

### 1. (c) Write short notes on (any two): [4+4 Marks]
#### (i) Postulates of molecular orbital theory.
- **Answer:**
  1. Atomic orbitals of comparable energies and proper symmetry combine linearly (LCAO) to form molecular orbitals (MOs).
  2. The number of molecular orbitals formed equals the number of combining atomic orbitals (one bonding MO and one anti-bonding MO per pair).
  3. Electrons fill molecular orbitals following the Aufbau principle, Pauli's exclusion principle, and Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity.
  4. Bonding MOs have lower energy and greater stability than parent atomic orbitals, whereas anti-bonding MOs have higher energy.

#### (ii) Pi ($\pi$) molecular orbital in 1,3-butadiene.
- **Answer:**
  - 1,3-Butadiene ($CH_2=CH-CH=CH_2$) has 4 unhybridized $2p$ orbitals yielding 4 molecular orbitals: $\psi_1, \psi_2$ (bonding) and $\psi_3^*, \psi_4^*$ (anti-bonding).
  - Four $\pi$-electrons occupy the lowest two MOs ($\psi_1^2 \psi_2^2$).
  - Delocalization over all 4 carbons confers extra resonance stabilization energy (delocalization energy).

#### (iii) Aromaticity in benzene.
- **Answer:**
  - **Hückel's Rule:** A planar, cyclic, fully conjugated ring system containing $(4n+2)\ \pi$ electrons (where $n=1$ for benzene, yielding $6\ \pi$ electrons) possesses aromatic stability.
  - High thermodynamic stability, resistance to addition reactions, and tendency to undergo electrophilic aromatic substitution.

### 1. (d) Discuss the crystal field theory for an octahedral complex with diagram. Why are octahedral complexes coloured? [8 Marks]
- **Answer:**
  - **Splitting in Octahedral Field:** In an octahedral coordination sphere, the 6 ligands approach along the Cartesian axes ($x, y, z$).
  - The degenerate $d$-orbitals split into two sets:
    - Lower energy triply degenerate $t_{2g}$ set ($d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{zx}$): stabilized by $-0.4 \Delta_o$.
    - Higher energy doubly degenerate $e_g$ set ($d_{x^2-y^2}, d_{z^2}$): destabilized by $+0.6 \Delta_o$.
  - **Origin of Colour:** When exposed to visible light, an electron in the lower energy $t_{2g}$ orbital absorbs a photon of energy corresponding to $\Delta_o$ and transitions to the vacant or half-filled $e_g$ orbital ($d$-$d$ transition). The complementary wavelength of the absorbed light is transmitted, imparting characteristic colour to the complex.

### What is CFSE? Calculate CFSE values in the $[Mn(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$ octahedral complex (high and low spin). [8 Marks]
- **Answer:**
  - **Crystal Field Stabilization Energy (CFSE):** The net energy decrease resulting from the distribution of $d$-electrons in the split crystal field orbitals compared to the spherical field.
    $$\text{CFSE} = [-0.4 \times n(t_{2g}) + 0.6 \times n(e_g)] \Delta_o + mP$$
  - For $[Mn(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$, Manganese is in $+2$ oxidation state with $d^5$ configuration.
  - **High Spin ($H_2O$ is a weak field ligand, $\Delta_o < P$):**
    - Configuration: $t_{2g}^3 e_g^2$
    - $\text{CFSE} = [-0.4(3) + 0.6(2)] \Delta_o = (-1.2 + 1.2)\Delta_o = 0\ \Delta_o$.
  - **Low Spin (in strong field, $\Delta_o > P$):**
    - Configuration: $t_{2g}^5 e_g^0$
    - $\text{CFSE} = [-0.4(5) + 0.6(0)] \Delta_o + 2P = -2.0 \Delta_o + 2P$.

## Unit-II

### 2. (a) Draw the line diagram of NMR spectrophotometer and mention its components. [4 Marks]
- **Answer:**
  **Components of NMR Spectrophotometer:**
  1. **Precision Magnet:** Generates a strong, stable, and homogeneous magnetic field ($B_0$).
  2. **Radio Frequency (RF) Transmitter:** Supplies electromagnetic radiation of radio frequency to induce nuclear spin transitions.
  3. **Sample Probe & Holder:** Holds the spinning NMR sample tube surrounded by transmitter/receiver coils.
  4. **RF Receiver & Detector:** Detects the resonant absorption/emission signals when nuclear spin resonance occurs.
  5. **Recorder / Data Processing Unit:** Amplifies and transforms signals into the output NMR spectrum (Chemical Shift $\delta$ vs Intensity).

### 2. (b) Explain various types of electronic transitions in UV-Vis Spectroscopy. Define chromophores and auxochrome giving suitable examples. [8 Marks]
- **Answer:**
  **Types of Electronic Transitions:**
  1. $\sigma \to \sigma^*$ (High energy, vacuum UV, e.g. alkanes like $CH_4$).
  2. $n \to \sigma^*$ (Intermediate energy, heteroatoms with lone pairs, e.g. $CH_3OH, CH_3Cl$).
  3. $\pi \to \pi^*$ (Lower energy, conjugated systems, e.g. alkenes, carbonyls).
  4. $n \to \pi^*$ (Lowest energy, weak intensity, e.g. ketones, aldehydes).

  **Definitions:**
  - **Chromophore:** A covalently unsaturated functional group that imparts colour or absorbs radiation in the UV-Vis region (e.g., $-C=C-, -C=O, -N=N-, -NO_2$).
  - **Auxochrome:** A saturated group containing non-bonding lone pairs that does not absorb by itself but when attached to a chromophore shifts the absorption to a longer wavelength (bathochromic/red shift) and increases intensity (e.g., $-OH, -NH_2, -OCH_3, -Cl$).

### 2. (c) Explain the different modes of vibration in a polyatomic molecule exposed to IR radiation. Write the formulae to calculate no. of vibrational modes in a non-linear molecule. [8 Marks]
- **Answer:**
  **Vibrational Modes:**
  1. **Stretching Vibrations:** Distance between two atoms increases or decreases along the bond axis.
     - *Symmetric stretching:* Both bonds stretch or compress simultaneously.
     - *Asymmetric stretching:* One bond stretches while the other compresses.
  2. **Bending (Deformation) Vibrations:** Bond angle changes without changing bond length.
     - *In-plane bending:* Scissoring (atoms move toward each other) and Rocking (atoms move in the same direction).
     - *Out-of-plane bending:* Wagging (atoms move up and down together) and Twisting (one atom moves up while other moves down).

  **Calculation Formula:**
  - For a non-linear molecule with $N$ atoms, Total Degrees of Freedom = $3N$.
  - Translational modes = 3, Rotational modes = 3.
  - **Number of Fundamental Vibrational Modes (Non-linear)** $= 3N - 6$.
  - *(Example for $H_2O$, $N=3 \implies 3(3) - 6 = 3$ vibrational modes).*
